About

Software should be boring to operate and interesting to read.

I'm Bansil Rakholiya, a full-stack engineer who spends most of his attention on the parts of a system nobody sees until they break: transaction boundaries, retry semantics, index strategy, memory behaviour under sustained load. My conviction is simple — the quality of a product is decided long before anyone opens a design file, in the shape of the data model and the honesty of its error handling.

My path started in C++, and that grounding never left. Manual memory management teaches you what abstractions actually cost. When I later moved into Java, C# and JavaScript, I carried the same instincts: measure before optimising, prefer explicitness over cleverness, and treat every allocation, query and network hop as something you must be able to justify.

On the backend I build domain-driven services with clear boundaries, idempotent write paths and schemas designed for the migrations they will inevitably need. On the frontend I care about semantics and accessibility first — a component that a screen reader cannot navigate is unfinished, regardless of how it renders. I write interfaces with real render budgets and design tokens rather than one-off styles.

Between those two ends sits the work I enjoy most: making the seams disappear. Contract-first APIs, typed boundaries, generated clients, deployment pipelines that make a release a non-event. I want the engineer who inherits my code eighteen months from now to find it obvious.
